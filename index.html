03 - Typography

Montserrat, set with intent

The whole site is Montserrat, which is in Shopify's font library, so it loads natively in Symmetry with no workaround. The fix for the thin look the site has now is weight, not a new font.

The "light font" fix: the current site reads thin because headings sit on Montserrat's lighter and extra light weights. Do not use the extra light version. Set headings to 600-800 and body to 400 (500 for small UI text), and bump base size and line height. That alone lifts the whole site from weedy to premium.
04 - Buttons

Orange leads, everything else supports

Primary actions are always the bright orange. Pill radius, Montserrat Medium, generous padding. Define once in Symmetry's button settings.

Primary: #FF8C00 fill, dark text. Secondary: ink outline, fills ink on hover.
On dark sections, keep the bright orange. Ghost buttons use a white outline.
Accessibility note: dark text on orange provides good contrast for buttons and large text. For small body text, don't put it on orange, use #242424 instead so it stays legible.
05 - Usage rules

Keep it consistent

Do

  • Use orange for CTAs, key icons, borders and small headlines, consistently.
  • Set type globally so every page inherits the same weights.
  • Alternate light, grey and dark schemes down a page for rhythm.
  • Keep main text in #242424 and secondary text in #434447.

Avoid

  • Leaning on red. It's an occasional accent, not a section default.
  • Headings on light Montserrat weights (the current thin look).
  • Small body text sitting on the orange.
  • Stacking equal white boxes, which is what reads as "boxy" now.
06 - Imagery

Three moods, one warm language

From the brand doc's photography guide. Across all three: warm directional natural light from the top right, the lens is the brightest element and appears to radiate, visible tactile texture (wood grain, woven fabric, soft bedding), shot at 85mm f/2.0 with a Kodak Portra 400 film tone. Always avoid cold blue lighting and white fluorescent or clinical light. The bright all-white "Bali-style" bedroom is ruled out for Sleep only, where the scene needs to stay dark with just the amber bedside lamp. Light and airy is welcome for Focus and Relax, think daylight whites for Focus and soft beige and taupe for Relax.

Notes for build. The three pillars run in cycle order, Focus, Relax, Sleep, each in its own brand colour and headed with the mood name and what sits in it, so a first-time visitor can see at a glance that the system runs across the whole day. Each one then leads with a feeling rather than a feature. Between them they cover the range of people Swanwick sells to, the screen-bound professional, the student or gamer, the parent who cannot switch off, and the light sleeper, without labelling anyone. The Build your system wording pushes the bundle rather than a single product. The trust row sits right by the button and leads with the science rather than a customer count, since that is the stronger proof for this audience. Each point is real and checkable: the Journal of Applied Psychology study run with the University of Washington and Indiana University and covered by Harvard Business Review, the independent lens transmission testing by Colts Laboratories, and the SleepScore validation. The per lens figures inside each pillar come straight from the Colts transmission reports, so they are safe to state. Two things to keep honest: the peer reviewed sleep outcome study was run on the Sleep glasses specifically, and the Focus figure of up to 98% is measured at 400nm and eases off across the rest of the range by design, so always state it that way rather than as a flat blanket number. The half a million customers figure still earns its place as social proof, better placed near reviews or the buy button than here. Trust is worth weaving into every section, not just this one.

08 - The wellness cycle

One idea, shaped to fit the screen

This is how we show that Focus, Relax and Sleep feed into each other. The content never changes from one device to the next. The layout is what adapts, so the loop reads clearly whether someone is on a laptop or holding a phone.

Mobile comes first here, not last. On a phone the three stages stack top to bottom as one scrolling column, with the return step curving back up the side, so the loop still reads as a loop on a narrow screen. On a wider screen the same three stages open out into a triangle. Same words, same colours, same order, only the arrangement moves. Every section in this document is meant to be built this way, designed for the small screen first and then allowed to breathe on larger ones, since most people reach Swanwick on a phone.
